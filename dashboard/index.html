A safety gateway that checks every command to a water plant against a physics model before it runs.

Backflow sits between remote access and the pump controller. A command that would overdose chlorine, overflow the tank or drain the town's pressure is held, and the operator on call decides.

The problem

A small water plant is run by a controller (a PLC) that a contractor or operator reaches remotely. The protocol it speaks, Modbus, has no idea who is sending a command or what the command will do. If the frame is well formed, the controller obeys.

On 30 July 2026 the FBI and EPA reported incidents in at least seven states in which attackers changed addresses and passwords on internet-facing PLCs at water and wastewater systems. Reported consequences included lost monitoring or control, pressure loss and flooding. Source 1: FBI / EPA public service announcement, 30 July 2026.
91% of US community water systems serve 10,000 people or fewer. Source 2: EPA report to Congress, November 2024, using fiscal year 2021 data (49,738 community water systems).

We built for the utility with one operator on call. A firewall can tell that operator whether a packet was allowed. It cannot tell them what the command would have done to the water.

How it works

  1. A command arrivesRemote access points at the gateway, not at the controller. Every Modbus write is parsed before anything is forwarded.
  2. Policy checkPlain code checks the register's allowed range, the size of the change, the source, the time of day and whether someone is physically at the cabinet.
  3. Physics checkA model of the tank and the chlorine dosing runs the command forward and reports what crosses a limit, and after how many minutes.
  4. Allow or holdA safe command is forwarded. An unsafe one is not: the sender gets a Modbus exception and the station never sees it. This decision takes milliseconds and involves no AI.
  5. The operator decidesThe operator gets one plain-language message with the reason and can approve or reject. An approval is checked against the physics model again before anything is released.
  6. The agent respondsAn AI agent investigates the held command, can block the source, restore the last known good settings or put the station in safe mode, and drafts the incident report. It cannot send arbitrary commands.

What the console shows for each command

ALLOW forwarded to the station
HOLD waiting for the operator
DENY refused outright

Two rules we hold ourselves to

No AI in the allow or hold path. The decision is made by policy code and the physics model. If the physics check fails or times out, the command is held.

The agent has no raw write tool. Its only actions are "revert to last known good", "enter safe mode" and "block a source". Releasing a held command needs the operator.

Who pays, and why

The buyer is the controls integrator. Small utilities do not buy security products. They pay the firm that installed their PLC and maintains it. Backflow is one box per station that the integrator installs and bills as a monthly line item. Cyber insurers and state grant programs are the second route.

Pricing idea. The box at hardware cost, plus a monthly fee per station in the range utilities already pay for remote monitoring. One published example of that range: Sensaphone lists cellular monitoring service for its Sentinel unit at $24.95 per month (source 4). This is our hypothesis. We have not sold a unit, and we have not validated the price with a buyer.

Why it is cheaper than an incident. A chlorine overdose or a drained tank means a do-not-drink or boil-water notice, flushing the system, lab samples, overtime, and a report to the state regulator. We do not have a sourced dollar figure for that, so we do not quote one. Our argument is the asymmetry: with Backflow, the same command costs the operator one message and one decision.

Limits of this build

This was built in one weekend. A judge should know where the edges are.

  • The plant model is a single well-mixed tank with demo parameters. It has not been calibrated against a real installation.
  • The chlorine alarm line is 4.0 mg/L, the EPA maximum residual disinfectant level (source 3). Compliance with that level is judged on a running annual average, so a crossing in our model is an engineering alarm, not a regulatory violation.
  • The overflow and low-pressure guards (95% and 15% tank level) are demo values, not site-specific limits.
  • The operator sign-in is a demo login: one shared account over plain HTTP on a local network.
  • Operator messages appear in the console. SMS delivery is not built.
